THE BIG PICTURE

AI-generated summary

Cells are the basic units of life. Every cell is enclosed by a plasma membrane, which controls what enters and leaves, and contains cytoplasm where many chemical reactions take place. Eukaryotic cells also contain membrane-bound organelles that divide specialized jobs between compartments. The nucleus stores most of the cell’s DNA, while ribosomes build proteins and mitochondria release usable energy from food molecules. Plant cells share these structures and also have a cellulose cell wall, chloroplasts for photosynthesis, and a large central vacuole that stores water and helps maintain shape.

✦

Remember: Cell structures work together. The membrane regulates exchange, DNA provides instructions, and organelles carry out specialized tasks.

CORE IDEAS

Key concepts

01

Cell theory

All living things are made of cells; cells are the basic unit of life; and new cells come from existing cells.

02

Structure supports function

Each organelle has a shape and role suited to a particular task, such as protein assembly or energy release.

03

Compartments organize cell work

Membrane-bound organelles let eukaryotic cells run different processes efficiently at the same time.

LANGUAGE TO KNOW

Important definitions

Organelle
A specialized structure inside a cell that performs a particular function.
Selective permeability
The property of a membrane that allows some substances to cross more easily than others.
Prokaryote
An organism whose cells do not have a membrane-bound nucleus.
Eukaryote
An organism whose cells contain a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles.
!

KEEP THESE HANDY

Important facts

  • Most eukaryotic DNA is stored in the nucleus.
  • Ribosomes assemble proteins from amino acids.
  • Mitochondria carry out most cellular respiration in eukaryotic cells.
  • Chloroplasts convert light energy into chemical energy in plants and algae.
